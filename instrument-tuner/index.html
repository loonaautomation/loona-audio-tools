Instrument Tuner - Chromatic Tuner Online Free

In short: the Instrument Tuner listens through your microphone and tells you the note you are playing and exactly how far off it is, in cents - green when you are within 5 cents of in tune - completely free, and nothing you play is ever uploaded or recorded.

Out-of-tune strings and instruments ruin practice and performances alike. Play one note at a time and this tuner names it, shows its frequency, and points the way: flat means tighten up, sharp means ease down, until the meter sits at zero. A standard-guitar-tuning strip is built in, and the chromatic engine works for any instrument or voice in its range.

How to use this tool

  1. Press "Start Tuner" and allow microphone permission - it is only asked for when you press the button.
  2. Play ONE note at a time, close to the microphone, and let it ring. Chords and background noise confuse any tuner.
  3. Read the note name and the cents meter: negative means flat (tighten / raise), positive means sharp (loosen / lower).
  4. Adjust a little at a time until the reading says "In tune" - within 5 cents - then move to the next string or note.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Instrument Tuner free?

Yes. It is completely free with no sign-up and no limit imposed by us.

How accurate is it - honestly?

In controlled tests with clean synthetic tones (sine, sawtooth and harmonic stacks from 82 Hz to 880 Hz), the detection engine read every tone within 3 cents and correctly reported a deliberate +25-cent detune as +25 cents. Real rooms add noise, and real instruments add strong harmonics, so treat the last few cents as guidance: play single sustained notes in a quiet room for the accuracy the tests showed.

Why does it sometimes show no note?

The tuner only reports a pitch when the signal is clearly periodic (a measured clarity above 0.9). Noise, chords, very short notes and sounds below about 30 Hz or above about 1,400 Hz are honestly reported as nothing, rather than guessed.

What is a cent?

One hundredth of a semitone. There are 100 cents between, for example, A and A#. Within about 5 cents of the target, most listeners hear a note as in tune.

Is my playing recorded or uploaded?

No. The microphone stream is analysed live on your device and discarded sample by sample. Nothing is recorded and nothing leaves the tab.

How it works

When you press Start, the page asks your browser - not a server - for the microphone, and analyses the sound with an autocorrelation pitch detector: it compares the waveform with shifted copies of itself to find the exact repeating period of your note, then refines that period with parabolic interpolation. The frequency becomes a note name and a cents deviation through the standard equal-temperament maths (A4 = 440 Hz).

A clarity score - how strongly the waveform repeats - gates every reading. Sustained single notes score above 0.95 in tests, white noise scores about 0.03, so the tuner shows a reading only when a real pitch is present. A short median filter over the last three readings keeps the display steady while you turn a tuning peg.

Worked example

A student picks up a guitar that has sat unused all winter. The low E string reads E2 but 38 cents flat. She tightens slowly, watching the meter walk from -38 to -12 to -3 - "In tune". Five strings later the whole guitar is ready, without any app, account or clip-on device.

More questions

Can it tune a piano?

It can check individual piano notes in its range, and piano tuners use far more specialised tools for a full tuning (pianos are tuned with stretched octaves). For checking whether a note is roughly on pitch, it works well.

Why does the reading jump between two notes?

Usually a strong harmonic is winning for a moment, or two sounds are ringing together. Mute the other strings, play one clean note, and let it sustain - the reading settles.

Does it work for singing?

Yes - sing one steady note close to the microphone and the tuner shows the note and cents, which is a classic way to check pitch matching.
