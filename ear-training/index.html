Ear Training - Interval Trainer Online Free

In short: the Ear Training interval trainer plays two notes and asks you to name the distance between them - with instant feedback, a running score and streak, and three difficulty levels - completely free, in your browser.

Recognising intervals by ear is the core skill behind singing in tune, transcribing songs and passing music exams. This trainer drills exactly that: a root note, a second note above it, and thirteen possible distances from a semitone to an octave - narrowed by the difficulty level you choose - with the answer explained in semitones every time.

You hear a first note, then a second note higher up. The distance between them is the interval. Beginners: the second note of "Happy Birthday" is a Major 2nd up; the first two notes of "Twinkle Twinkle" are a Perfect 5th apart in pitch class - anchor songs like these are how musicians learn intervals.

Private by design: the Ear Training sounds are generated locally in your browser with the Web Audio API. There is no microphone, no recording and nothing is uploaded - close the tab and everything is gone.

How to use this tool

  1. Choose a difficulty: Easy reaches a Perfect 4th, Medium a Minor 6th, Hard the full octave.
  2. Press "New Question" and listen: a first note, then a higher second note.
  3. Click the interval you think you heard. The correct answer turns green either way, with the semitone count explained.
  4. Use Replay to hear the pair again, or Play Together to hear the two notes as one chord - then press New Question and build your streak.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Ear Training trainer free?

Yes. It is completely free with no sign-up and no limit imposed by us.

What is an interval?

The distance in pitch between two notes, measured in semitones. A Perfect 5th is 7 semitones - the distance from C to G. The trainer covers all thirteen intervals from Minor 2nd (1 semitone) to Perfect Octave (12).

How were the questions and answers checked?

The question generator and interval table are tested code: over 500 generated questions, every target note is exactly the chosen number of semitones above the root, in both MIDI numbers and actual frequencies, and every name matches the standard interval table.

Are the intervals always ascending?

Yes - the second note is always higher than the first in this trainer. Ascending intervals are the standard starting point; descending and harmonic (together) recognition build on them, and the Play Together button gives you a first taste of the harmonic form.

How do I actually get better?

Anchor songs: give each interval a song you know that starts with it - a semitone is the Jaws theme, a Perfect 5th is Twinkle Twinkle, an octave is Somewhere Over the Rainbow. Short daily sessions beat occasional long ones.

How it works

Each question is generated by tested code: a root note is chosen in a comfortable range (A3 to F4), an interval size is drawn within your difficulty, and the target note is computed exactly that many semitones above the root in equal temperament. Both notes are played as clean triangle-wave tones with a soft envelope through the Web Audio API - no samples, nothing downloaded.

Scoring is plain bookkeeping: asked, correct and current streak, kept only in this tab. Close the page and the score resets - it is practice, not an account.

Worked example

A choir student keeps missing the jump to the fifth in a new song. Ten minutes on Medium difficulty - most questions land between 5 and 8 semitones - and her score goes from guessing to 8 correct in a row, with the Perfect 5th recognised instantly every time it appears.

More questions

Why do two intervals sound almost the same to me?

Neighbouring intervals (Major 3rd vs Perfect 4th, for example) are close in size. Drop to Easy until the small set is reliable, use Play Together to feel the pair as one sound, then widen the difficulty again.

Can I train with my own instrument?

Yes - after hearing a question, find the two notes on your instrument before answering. Matching the trainer on a real instrument is excellent combined practice.

Is my score saved anywhere?

No. The score lives only in this tab and disappears when you close it. Nothing about your practice is stored or sent anywhere.
