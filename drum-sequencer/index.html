Drum Sequencer - Online Beat Maker Free

In short: the Drum Sequencer lets you build a drum beat on a 16-step grid - kick, snare, closed and open hi-hat, all synthesized live in your browser with no samples - with rock, house and hip-hop presets and tempo control, completely free.

Every beat you have ever tapped along to is a pattern of hits on a grid. This sequencer gives you that grid: sixteen steps (one bar of sixteenth notes), four classic drum sounds made from pure synthesis - a pitch-dropping sine for the kick, filtered noise for the snare and hats - and presets that show how rock, house and hip-hop place their hits differently.

Steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 are the four main beats of the bar - most kick and snare patterns are built around them. All four sounds (kick, snare, closed hi-hat, open hi-hat) are synthesized live in your browser; there are no audio samples and nothing is downloaded.

Private by design: the Drum Sequencer synthesizes every sound locally in your browser with the Web Audio API. There are no samples to download, no microphone and nothing is uploaded - close the tab and everything is gone.

How to use this tool

  1. Pick a preset to hear a finished pattern, or start tapping steps on the grid - each row is one drum sound, each column one sixteenth-note step.
  2. Press Play. The current step is outlined in gold as the bar loops.
  3. Change steps, tempo and volume while it plays - changes apply as the loop reaches them.
  4. Use Clear Grid to start from silence and build your own pattern from the four main beats (steps 1, 5, 9, 13) outwards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Drum Sequencer free?

Yes. It is completely free with no sign-up and no limit imposed by us.

Are the drum sounds samples?

No - every sound is synthesized live. The kick is a sine wave whose pitch drops from 150 Hz to about 42 Hz in a tenth of a second (the classic electronic kick recipe); the snare is band-filtered noise plus a short tone; the hi-hats are high-passed noise, short for closed and longer for open. That is why the page loads instantly with nothing to download.

What does 16 steps mean?

One bar divided into sixteenth notes. At 120 BPM each step is exactly 0.125 seconds, so steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 land on the four main beats of the bar.

Why does the pattern keep looping?

A step sequencer is a loop machine by design - one bar repeats so you can build and judge a groove. Change anything and you hear the change on the next pass.

Can I save or download my beat?

Not from this page - it is a live instrument for playing and learning patterns. To record a beat, play it and capture it with the Screen Recorder on this site with sound, or note the grid down: each preset and pattern here is just 64 on/off steps.

How it works

The grid is stored as four rows of sixteen on/off steps - exactly the pattern format the page shares with its tested pattern engine, which expands a pattern into timed events (at 120 BPM, step 8 is at exactly 1.0 second). Playback uses a look-ahead scheduler on the Web Audio clock: sounds are synthesised and placed slightly ahead of time, so the groove stays tight even if the screen is busy.

The four sounds are synthesis recipes, not recordings. That keeps the page at zero downloads, zero samples and identical sound on every device.

Worked example

A music teacher wants her class to hear how genres differ without any equipment. She loads Rock (kick on 1 and 9, snare on 5 and 13), then House (kick on every beat) at the same tempo, then Hip-Hop with its busier hats - three grids, three grooves, and the class can see and hear exactly what changed.

More questions

Why is there no bassline or melody?

This tool is the drum machine only - rhythm first. Melodic instruments would be a separate tool; keeping this page to four drum sounds keeps it instant and easy to learn.

The beat sounds different at very high or low volume - why?

Small speakers cannot reproduce the kick fundamental (around 42-150 Hz) faithfully, so the kick sounds thinner on phone speakers than on headphones. That is physics, not a fault in the pattern.

Can I make odd time signatures?

The grid is one bar of 16 sixteenth steps. Leaving the last steps of a row empty creates shorter loops only if all rows agree - for true odd metres, the Metronome on this site covers beats per bar directly.
