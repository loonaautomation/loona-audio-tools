BPM Detector - Find a Song's Tempo Online Free

In short: the BPM Detector finds the tempo of a song or recording in beats per minute, shows how confident the detection is, and tells you honestly when a file has no clear beat at all - completely free, and your file never leaves your device.

DJs lining up two tracks, teachers picking music at the right pace for an activity, dancers and editors counting beats - all of them start with one number: BPM. How to use this tool

  1. Click the box above or drag your audio file into it - MP3, WAV, M4A and OGG all work. Analysis starts automatically; files of 10 seconds or more give the most reliable result.
  2. Read the detected tempo, the confidence note, the seconds per beat, and the estimated number of beats in the file.
  3. If the note says no clear beat was found, believe it - speech and ambient recordings often have no steady tempo, and the number shown is only the strongest pattern.
  4. Optional check: play the track and tap the "Tap on the Beat" button on every beat to compare your own tempo with the detector.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the BPM Detector free?

Yes. It is completely free with no sign-up and no limit imposed by us.

Are my audio files uploaded to a server?

No. Your file is decoded and analysed 100% inside your browser with the Web Audio API. It never leaves your device.

How accurate is the detection?

On music with a clear, steady beat - drum tracks, pop, electronic music, metronome-like recordings - controlled tests with known tempos from 60 to 174 BPM were detected exactly. On complex real music the strongest pattern can occasionally be the half or double of the tempo you feel, which is why the page shows a confidence rating and includes a tap-tempo check.

What does the confidence number mean?

It compares the strength of the winning beat pattern with the average pattern strength across all tempos. Clear beat tracks score 5 or higher; the page calls a result a clear beat from 2.5 upwards. A steady tone or speech scores around 1 - no real beat.

Which tempos can it detect?

Between 40 and 208 BPM, in half-BPM steps. Almost all songs, from slow ballads to fast electronic tracks, fall inside that range.

How it works

Your file is opened right inside your browser and decoded into raw sound samples with the Web Audio API - nothing is uploaded to any server. The tool builds an onset envelope: a curve of how sharply the sound energy rises moment by moment, which spikes wherever a drum hit, note attack or other beat event happens. It then measures that curve at every tempo from 40 to 208 BPM (a tempogram) and reports the tempo whose rhythm matches the spikes most strongly.

The confidence rating is part of the answer, not decoration: when no tempo stands out - as with speech, a steady tone, or freely timed music - the tool says so plainly instead of inventing a number.

Worked example

A dance teacher wants class music at about 120 BPM. She drops three candidate songs into the detector one by one: the first reads 122 BPM with a clear beat, the second 96 BPM, and the third is an interview recording that honestly reports no clear beat. She picks the first song, seconds per beat 0.492, and plans her routine on it.

More questions

Why did it report double (or half) the tempo I expected?

Some rhythms repeat strongly at two levels at once - for example a track with both a slow kick and a fast hat pattern. The detector reports the level whose pattern is strongest overall. The tap tool on the page lets you confirm the level you feel.

Can it detect the tempo of a live performance?

Save or record the performance as a file first - the Voice Recorder on this site can do that - then load the file here. The detector needs a finished recording to analyse.

Why does my speech recording show a BPM at all?

It shows the strongest repeating pattern it could find, labelled with low confidence and a clear "no clear beat" note. Treat that number as meaningless for speech - the honest reading is the confidence note, not the number.
