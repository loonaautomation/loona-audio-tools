Noise Generator - White, Pink & Brown Noise Online Free

In short: the Noise Generator makes the three classic colours of noise - bright white, balanced pink and deep brown - for sleep, focus and masking distracting sounds, played live in your browser or downloaded as WAV, completely free.

A fan that is not there, rain that never stops, a rumble that swallows the street outside - steady noise helps people sleep, study and work by covering sounds that change. This tool is deliberately different from the Tone Generator on this site: that tool makes a pitched tone at one exact frequency, while this tool makes unpitched noise spread across all frequencies, shaped three different ways.

For looping: play the download in any player set to repeat - the noise is statistically steady from the first second to the last, so a loop sounds continuous. Start at low volume, especially with headphones.

Private by design: the Noise Generator creates every sound locally in your browser with the Web Audio API. Nothing is recorded, uploaded or stored - close the tab and everything is gone.

How to use this tool

  1. Choose the noise type: white for masking sharp sounds and speech, pink for balanced rain-like focus and sleep, brown for a deep calming rumble. The description under the menu explains each one.
  2. Press "Play Noise" - start with the volume LOW, especially on headphones - and adjust to a comfortable level.
  3. To keep the sound beyond this tab, choose a download length and press "Download as WAV".
  4. Play the downloaded WAV in any player with repeat/loop switched on for continuous noise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Noise Generator free?

Yes. It is completely free with no sign-up and no limit imposed by us.

What is the difference between white, pink and brown noise?

It is about how energy is spread across frequencies. White noise has equal energy at every frequency and sounds bright and hissy. Pink noise drops 3 dB per octave, so each octave sounds equally loud - balanced, like rain. Brown noise drops 6 dB per octave and piles energy into the bass - a deep rumble.

How is this different from the Tone Generator?

Completely different job: the Tone Generator makes one pitched tone at an exact frequency (a test signal with a note-like pitch). The Noise Generator makes unpitched, broadband noise - thousands of frequencies at once, shaped by colour - for sleep, focus and masking. Neither tool does the other one's job.

Which colour is best for sleep?

Most people prefer pink or brown for sleep because the reduced high frequencies feel softer over hours. White masks sudden sharp sounds best. Try each for a few minutes at low volume - the best one is the one you stop noticing.

Is it safe for babies and for my hearing?

Keep the volume modest - about the level of a quiet conversation or less, and never place a speaker close to a baby's ears. Noise at sensible volume is widely used in nurseries; loud sound of any kind, for long periods, is not good for anyone's hearing.

How it works

Your chosen noise is generated sample by sample on your device. White noise is pure randomness. Pink noise passes that randomness through the classic Kellet filter bank, which tilts its energy down 3 dB per octave. Brown noise integrates it with a gentle leak, tilting down 6 dB per octave. All three are generated by tested code whose statistics are verified: the correlation and zero-crossing fingerprints of the three colours come out in exactly the expected order (brown smoothest, white roughest), and no sample ever clips.

Playback loops a generated buffer; downloads render a fresh buffer of exactly the length you chose and save it as standard WAV with the same tested encoder the Audio Cutter uses.

Worked example

A teacher marking exams at a noisy kitchen table puts pink noise on low through one earbud - the clatter and conversation blur into the background, and the steady rain-like sound lets her finish the pile in one sitting.

More questions

Why does the download not last for hours?

An hour of WAV noise would be a file of hundreds of megabytes for a sound that repeats statistically every second. A 30 or 60 second WAV on loop gives the identical listening result at a tiny size.

Can I mix noise with music or lessons?

Yes - play the noise quietly underneath any other audio. Many people keep it well below the main sound, just enough to fill the gaps between distracting noises.

Does the noise keep playing if I close the tab?

No - like everything on this site, the sound exists only in this tab. For overnight use, download the WAV and play it in a player app instead.
